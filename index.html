Quantum jumps of sound

A working model of Stanford’s 2026 experiment. A qubit asks a vibrating resonator “one phonon, or none?” hundreds of times over, without destroying the answer, until the phonon leaves. Put your own resonator’s lifetime on the dial and see whether it would show.

From the paper (Makihara, Szakiel et al., Science, 17 Sept 2026, doi 10.1126/science.aeh7535): a phonon lifetime of 2.1 ms, a qubit shift of 328 kHz per phonon, and single phonons heralded at 85% fidelity. Everything else here is an assumption, and is labelled as one.

From your BVD fit to a quantum coupling

The static and motional capacitances from an ordinary Butterworth–Van Dyke fit are enough to estimate how hard a resonator grabs a qubit, and so the shift per phonon. Any FBAR admittance fit gives you both.

Why quantum labs shrink the transducer: C₀ sits straight across the qubit, and the qubit’s nonlinearity falls as one over the total capacitance. A 50 Ω-matched 2 GHz FBAR brings about 1.6 pF; a qubit wants tens of femtofarads.

What is being simulated

The resonator is tracked by its phonon number, which falls at rate n(nth+1)/T₁ and climbs at rate (n+1)nth/T₁. Each reading asks only “how many phonons?” and leaves that number alone, so the quantum state between readings stays a mixture of number states and the full quantum trajectory reduces to this jump process. The quantum part is in the steps, and in choosing to measure energy rather than displacement.

One reading

A qubit pulse tuned to the one-phonon line, long enough (one over the shift) to pick that line out, then a readout. It goes wrong when the qubit decays mid-reading, when neighbouring lines leak into the pulse, or when the readout errs.

The estimate

The yellow trace is a Bayes filter run over the readings as they arrive, which is what the lab watches live. A herald needs 99:1 odds; jump times and lifetimes use the whole record. When the mode is warm the one-phonon state can also leave upwards, so it lives T₁ ÷ (1 + 3nth).

Limits

From the paper: 2.1 ms, 328 kHz per phonon, 85% herald fidelity. The qubit lifetime, readout error, timings, temperature and the bridge examples are assumptions. Line widths and pulse selectivity are simple Lorentzian estimates; there is no pulse shaping and no back-action on the phonon. Bridge formulas: g ≈ (f/2)√(Cₘ/Ctotal), α = −e²/(2Ctotalh), 2χ = 2g²α/[Δ(Δ+α)], valid for Cₘ ≪ Ctotal and g ≪ |Δ|, |Δ+α|. Source code and physics tests: github.com/ash23x/quantum-jumps-of-sound.
